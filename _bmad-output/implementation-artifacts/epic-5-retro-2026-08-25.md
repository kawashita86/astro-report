**Finding A — Epic 5 doubled the surface of a known, still-open cross-story defect
(epic-4-retro-item-23).**
`core/gate/run.py` / `shell/runner/driver.py:536-607` (`_run_gate_passed`'s pass branch): on a Gate
pass, `store_report(session, ...)` and `store_gate_result(session, ...)` are called back-to-back, each
doing its own `add()` + `flush()` (never a commit) before `drive()`'s single `session.commit()` at the
end of the stage. This is the exact "with_backoff-retried two-write stage function" shape
epic-4-retro-item-23 already flags as open — `_run_payload_ready` (store_report_payload +
store_report_theme) was the first instance. Story 5.6's own Boundaries section explicitly names
item-23 and marks it "already open, pre-existing and out of scope here" — a conscious, documented
deferral, not an oversight. But the epic still added a *second* occurrence of the same pattern rather
than avoiding it, so the number of places a mid-stage flush failure can leave the SQLAlchemy session in
a pending-rollback state (which `with_backoff`'s blind retry-any-exception and `drive()`'s
`except`-block `session.add(run); session.commit()` do not guard against) has grown from one to two in
this codebase area alone, one of them now sitting directly on the Gate's own write-once-per-check
invariant Story 5.6 was built to guarantee.
Source: `shell/runner/driver.py:593-607`, `_bmad-output/implementation-artifacts/spec-5-6-keep-the-gate-s-record-so-a-regression-is-visible-early.md:59-64`, `sprint-status.yaml` action item `epic-4-retro-item-23-guard-with_backoff-retried-two-write-sta`.

**Finding B (lower-confidence, flagged for awareness) — the day-of-month vocabulary token can
false-match a non-date bare numeral.**
`core/gate/run.py:197-199` (`_asserted_days_of_month`) applies `vocabulary.day_of_month_pattern`
(`\b([1-9]|[12][0-9]|3[01])\b`, per Story 5.1's Design Notes) to the *entire lowered sentence text*, not
just a date-shaped substring. Any bare 1-31 numeral anywhere in a Claim sentence — a duration ("per i
prossimi 3 giorni"), an unrelated count — is classified as an asserted day-of-month fact and checked
against the cited entries' calendar day. This is inherited, documented behavior from Story 5.1 ("Day-of-
month numeral... is_claim(...) is True", `spec-5-1...md`'s I/O matrix) and Story 5.2's Never section
("no degree checking — out of scope by design"), so it is not a hidden defect — but the style guide
(`data/style-guide.seed.md:121-124`) mandates dates always be written as "day-number plus month name,"
which somewhat contains the risk for genuine dates; the residual risk is any other bare 1-31 number the
Generator writes (a duration, a count) triggering a false `invented_fact`/`contradicted_fact` and
burning a regeneration attempt (Story 5.4) for a sentence that made no date claim at all. Disposition:
accept as-is / defer — already a known, documented tradeoff, but worth a note if false Gate failures
are observed in practice.

**Finding 3 (defer, process lesson) — the day-of-month and `casa`+ordinal Claim triggers can false-match
ordinary, non-astrological Italian.**
`core/gate/run.py:186-199` (`_asserted_houses`/`_asserted_days_of_month`). `day_of_month_pattern`
(`\b([1-9]|[12][0-9]|3[01])\b`, Story 5.1) is applied to the *entire* lowered sentence text, so any bare
1-31 number — a duration ("per i prossimi 3 giorni"), a count, an age — is classified as an asserted
day-of-month fact and checked against cited entries' calendar days. Independently, "casa" (ordinary
Italian for "home") plus an ordinal (`seconda`, `prima`, ...) is common in mundane, non-astrological
Italian ("la mia seconda casa al mare" — "my second home at the beach") and would be classified as a
house Claim requiring a Payload citation by lexical co-occurrence alone. Both are inherited, *documented*
design choices from Story 5.1 (its own I/O matrix: "Day-of-month numeral... `is_claim(...) is True`")
and Story 5.2's Never section ("no degree checking — out of scope by design"), so this is not a hidden
defect — the closed-vocabulary design accepts false positives as the cost of a mechanically-checkable
Claim boundary. The residual risk is unnecessary regenerations (Story 5.4) or, at the bound, terminal
failures for prose that made no astronomical Claim at all. The style guide
(`data/style-guide.seed.md:121-124`) mandates dates always be written as "day-number plus month name,"
which contains genuine-date false negatives somewhat, but not the duration/count/mundane-"casa" cases.
No test in either `tests/test_gate_classify.py` or `tests/test_gate_run.py` exercises a plausible
mundane sentence with a bare number or a "casa"+ordinal combination that isn't astrological. Verified
independently by the adversarial and edge-case lenses (both raised it; adversarial supplied the "casa"
example). Source: `core/gate/run.py:186-199`; `core/gate/vocabulary.it.json`;
`spec-5-1-define-what-counts-as-a-claim-as-versioned-data.md`'s I/O matrix.

**Finding 4 (defer) — a future vocabulary revision can silently under-check a newly recognized Claim
category.**
`core/gate/run.py:70-121` hardcodes `_BODY_MAP`/`_SIGN_MAP`/`_CASA_ORDINAL_TO_HOUSE` as Italian→English/
house-number translation tables, separate from `core/gate/vocabulary.it.json`'s own word lists. Nothing
ties them together: `shell/gate.py`'s loader validates the JSON's shape but never checks that every
`planets`/`signs`/`casa_ordinals` word has a corresponding entry in these hardcoded maps. If a future
vocabulary revision adds a word to one of these three categories without a matching code change,
`classify.py::is_claim()` correctly flags the sentence as a Claim (vocabulary-driven), but
`run.py`'s per-category fact extraction silently has no translation for it — the Claim is checked as an
empty asserted set, potentially passing ungrounded rather than failing loud. Source: `core/gate/run.py:
70-121,173-194`; `shell/gate.py`'s loader (no cross-check against `run.py`'s maps, by construction — the
loader can't reference `core/`, but nothing else enforces the pairing either).

**Finding 5 (defer) — `_date_facts` lacks the defensive guard its sibling fact-extraction functions
have.**
`core/gate/run.py:243-252` calls `datetime.fromisoformat(value).day` directly after only an
`isinstance(value, str)` check — unlike `_body_sign_facts`/`_house_facts`, which isinstance-check every
field and silently skip anything malformed. `run_gate()`'s own docstring claims it is pure and never
raises for any `(draft, payload, vocabulary)` triple; a malformed non-ISO date string in a cited entry
(a Payload data-quality bug, not expected in practice since `freeze_payload()` is internally trusted)
would crash `run_gate()` with an unhandled `ValueError` instead of degrading to a Gate violation,
funneling what should be the Gate's own reporting path into a generic stage failure instead. Source:
`core/gate/run.py:243-252` vs. `211-240` (the three sibling functions' isinstance-guard pattern).

**Finding 6 (defer, minor) — `gate_passed` is retried by `with_backoff`'s default 3 attempts despite
`run_gate()` being deterministic.**
`shell/runner/driver.py:121-123` (`_STAGE_BACKOFF_OVERRIDES`) has no entry for `gate_passed`, so every
Gate failure pays for up to 2 extra, guaranteed-identical `run_gate()` recomputations plus
`with_backoff`'s small sleeps (0.1s + 0.2s, `_BASE_DELAY_SECONDS`) before `drive()`'s
`except GateFailedError` branch ever sees it — negligible in wall-clock terms today, but the design
intent (a deterministic function gains nothing from retry) is not reflected in the override table.
Source: `shell/runner/driver.py:121-123,701`; `shell/runner/backoff.py:25`.

**Finding 7 (defer) — `_run_draft_ready`'s attempt-tagged write has no guard against a concurrent
`drive()` race.**
`drive()` is called from both the start route and the poll route with no row-level locking
(`shell/runner/driver.py`; `shell/http/routes/report_runs.py`). Two concurrent `drive()` calls for the
same run could both read `run.regeneration_count == N` and both call `store_report_draft(attempt=N)`;
the composite unique index on `(report_run_id, attempt)` (Story 5.4) would reject the second as an
`IntegrityError`, which is not specifically handled and would fall into `drive()`'s generic
`except Exception` path, incrementing `stage_failure_count` for what is actually a harmless duplicate
request. This is the same class of gap as the still-open `epic-4-retro-item-26` (a concurrent
`IntegrityError` on `ReportPayload.report_run_id`'s unique constraint), now with a second instance on
`ReportDraft`'s new composite constraint. Source: `shell/runner/driver.py:526-533`;
`shell/adapters/postgres/report_draft.py`'s `(report_run_id, attempt)` index (Story 5.4).

## Acceptance verdict

**accepted-with-open-items** (criteria: **declared** — epic-5-context.md's Requirements & Constraints
and each story's own Acceptance Criteria).

All 6 stories are `done`, `pending_stories` is empty, and every declared Acceptance Criterion across
the six stories was checked against the current code and found satisfied: the vocabulary is versioned
and closed (5.1); `run_gate()` is pure/deterministic and checks the four fact categories plus the

queries are proven directly computable (5.6). Cascade ordering, immutability guards, and the
write-once-per-check bookkeeping (verified in code, not just tests) all match their specs.

No finding contradicts a declared Acceptance Criterion outright — the emotional core promise ("send a
report without reading it first") is not broken by any finding here in the common case. But Finding 1
is a genuine, concretely-evidenced gap inside the epic's own stated scope (Story 5.5 named it, Story
5.6 built the fix, the wiring was never finished), and Finding 2 shows the epic knowingly expanded a
still-open, previously-flagged defect's surface area onto the Gate's own write-once invariant. Both are
narrow-window risks (a vocabulary edit landing in the exact gap between a terminal failure and its

breakage, which is why the verdict is **accepted-with-open-items** rather than **rejected** — but they
